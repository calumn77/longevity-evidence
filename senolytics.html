Senolytics

Can removing senescent cells improve health as we age?

Early human clinical research
Provisional conclusion: Strong preclinical rationale and early human signals, but clinical benefits remain uncertain and human lifespan extension has not been demonstrated.

What are senolytics?

Senolytics are interventions intended to selectively remove senescent cells. Senescence can have beneficial roles, including wound healing and tumour suppression; indiscriminate removal may therefore carry risks.

Why might they work?

Some senescent cells release inflammatory and tissue-modifying molecules. Removing certain populations improved outcomes in selected mouse studies. Results cannot automatically be extrapolated to humans.

Evidence by stage

StageAssessment
MechanisticSubstantial support in selected models
AnimalStrong in some disease and aging models
Human signalsSmall studies report changes in senescence-associated markers
Clinical outcomesEarly trials; insufficient replicated evidence of general benefit

Safety and limitations

Effects differ by compound and condition. Some candidate senolytics are prescription medicines with serious potential adverse effects. Long-term safety, selectivity and optimal dosing remain uncertain. This page does not recommend self-experimentation.

What would change our assessment?

Independent, adequately powered randomized trials showing meaningful clinical benefits, with long-term safety monitoring and evidence that observed effects result from senescent-cell clearance.

Starting references

Baker et al., 2016 — Naturally occurring p16-positive cells shorten healthy lifespan

Hickson et al., 2019 — Senolytics decrease senescent cells in humans
